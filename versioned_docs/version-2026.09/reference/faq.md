## Does regular Terminal-Bench have the same proposal form?

Not in the pinned contribution guide. It asks for early maintainer feedback through `#tb-task-spam`, email, or conversation. TB-Science uses the structured Airtable form.

## Can an independent researcher submit?

Use truthful affiliation and role information. For TB-Science, state “Independent Researcher” when appropriate and provide the strongest real evidence of domain competence. Approval depends on task quality and credible authorship, not a fabricated institution.

## Can a medical doctor author a science task?

Yes when they materially design and validate a medicine/health-science workflow within their competence and agree to be a named, reachable author. A degree alone does not validate unrelated computational or scientific claims.

## Do I need publications?

The TB-Science form requests academic work. Provide real relevant work. If none exists and the field permits a truthful “none” response, explain other concrete experience. Never attribute someone else’s work to yourself.

## Can I recommend someone I do not know as reviewer?

The field is optional. You may list notable domain experts as suggestions without implying endorsement, but leaving it blank is safer than speculative or conflicted recommendations.

## Does a private GitHub repository make the website private?

No. Hosting access is separate. This portal’s design uses Cloudflare Access in front of the production hostname; repository privacy protects source access, not the deployed URL by itself.

## Can we disable internet to make a regular task harder?

No. Current regular Terminal-Bench guidance explicitly treats it as open internet and says lack of network is not a valid difficulty mechanism.

## Is Oracle pass enough?

No. You also need NOP failure, alternate-valid acceptance, negative and mutation rejection, ground-truth isolation, and repeated stability.

## Should hidden tests contain hidden requirements?

No. Hide cases and values, not the behavioral contract.
